Ferrocene into Albumin

A second demo for the metal-docking package — and the step the first one quietly skips.

Receptor 1AO6 Ligand Fe(C₅H₅)₂ Site Sudlow I 25 August 2026

Why a second demo

The package already shipped a worked example: the 1JZI case from the MetalDock paper, a rhenium complex docked into azurin. It is a redocking — the complex is taken out of the crystal it came from and put back. The binding site is therefore known before the run begins, and success is scored as RMSD against the crystallographic pose.

That is the right way to validate a method. It is not what a user does.

A user has a protein and a complex that has never been co-crystallised with it, and wants to know where — if anywhere — it binds. Nothing in a redocking teaches the step that question turns on.

Suggested by Sylvestre Bonnet, PI of the MetalDock tool, who pointed out that the demo should dock into a protein with no metal complex bound, and named human serum albumin as the system to do it with.

The case

Receptor
1AO6 · albumin · chain A
Ligand
Ferrocene, xtb-optimised
Site
Sudlow I · subdomain IIA
Box
20 Å cube

Chain A only. 1AO6 contains two identical copies; albumin is monomeric in solution, so the second is a crystallographic artefact. Docking into a spurious dimer interface is an easy way to get a confident wrong answer.

The receptor is genuinely apo. Its only HETATM records are seven waters, which the cleaning step removes. There is no complex to strip and no pose to lean on.

The ligand is independent. Fe–C comes out at 2.064–2.066 Å against roughly 2.04–2.06 Å measured for ferrocene — the check that the input is sound before any docking happens.

Choosing the site

AutoDock requires a grid centre. It accepts auto, which means the receptor's centre of mass — almost never a binding site. This package's own default centres on the ligand's metal atom, which is only meaningful when the ligand's coordinates came out of the receptor's crystal.

For a prediction, neither default is usable. You must choose. Two attempts were made, and the difference between them is the reason this report exists.

Attempt 1 · rejected

The centroid of Trp214

Trp214 lines Sudlow I and is the residue everyone names when describing it. Its centroid looks like a reasonable centre.

Contacts328–354 · IIB

Attempt 2 · shipped

The position of a co-crystallised ligand

R-warfarin is the canonical Sudlow I probe. Its centroid, taken from 2BXD and superposed into the 1AO6 frame.

Best ΔG−3.00 kcal/mol
Spread across poses0.01
Contacts11/12 in IIA

A residue centroid is not a pocket centroid. A co-crystallised ligand is.

The transfer is verifiable rather than asserted: all 578 Cα atoms matched between the two structures, and the superposition RMSD is 0.88 Å — what you expect for the same protein in two crystal forms. Had it come back at 5 Å, the alignment would have been wrong.

Where no co-crystal exists, use a pocket detector — fpocket, P2Rank, CASTp — rather than guessing from sequence.

Result

poses 10   best −3.00   median −3.00   spread 0.01 kcal/mol

contacts 12, of which 11 in subdomain IIA:
  TYR150  ARG222  LEU238  ARG257  LEU260  ALA261
  ILE264  LYS286  SER287  HIS288  ILE290  ALA291

Those are the residues the albumin literature names for Sudlow site I. The energy is modest because ferrocene is small and neutral; the pose is the result worth reading, not the number. All ten poses fall within 0.01 kcal/mol of one another — a single well-defined minimum, where the misplaced box gave a 0.45 kcal/mol scatter.

rmsd_values comes back empty, deliberately. No crystallographic pose exists to compare against. Leaving the 1JZI reference in place would have compared a ferrocene pose to a rhenium complex and printed a number that looks like validation and means nothing.

What else this established

Iron takes a different code path from rhenium. It sits in INTERNAL_PARAM_METALS, so no Lennard-Jones terms are written and docking relies on atom_par Fe from the parameter library. Before this demo that branch had never been executed — iron was declared supported and unit-tested at the API level, but no docking run had used it.

It works. xtb converges CM5 charges on Fe(II), and the graph builder resolves η⁵ sandwich bonding from covalent radii alone: 30 bonds for 21 atoms, being 10 C–H, 10 C–C and 10 Fe–C.
